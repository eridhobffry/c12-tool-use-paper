I2 Name the two sets in 44.3 and 34.9

I4 The decision sits in different places

ReAct switching rules Confidence 86% Complexity Low

Description One comparative clause says who is allowed to stop. In the ReAct combinations, an outside rule switches method when a step limit is reached or the majority vote is low. The step limit, 7 on HotpotQA and 5 on FEVER, is a setting. In Toolformer, the same model may start a call when the call token is among its top-k next tokens, and the reported evaluation allowed at most one call per input. At k=1 that token has to be the single most likely next token. The notes say the majority vote is low. They do not give a fraction of the 21 samples, so that fraction stays out until it is checked against the PDF again. The sentence also avoids "the model can only make one call." ReAct can chain calls until it answers or hits the limit.

Basis direct: notes/handoff-2026-11-20.md:31 — "ReAct's combinations switch method by a rule: a step limit is reached, or the majority vote is low." notes/handoff-2026-11-20.md:50 — "k means the model may start a call when the call token is among its top-k next tokens. Toolformer allows at most one call per input in the evaluation." notes/handoff-2026-11-20.md:80 — "Do not write 'the model can only make one call.'"

Rationale Without this clause the paragraph is two adjacent facts. With it, a classmate can say where each decision lives. The clause uses the notes' own wording for k and for the one-call evaluation.

Downsides It spends a sentence on a distinction the body sections may already have taught. It does not say which ReAct trigger fires in which direction. The checked notes list the triggers with "or" and do not assign directions.
